### 1. The Deleted Code: Why you cannot toggle "Any" Emotion
In your analogy, you ask if one can turn "on/off" any emotional state. In Theravada, Liberation (*Vimutti*) is not merely the *suppression* of defilements (which can be toggled); it is their *eradication*.

Buddhaghosa explains this through the concept of **Samuccheda-pahāna** (Abandoning by cutting off). When one reaches the final stage of enlightenment (Arahantship):

*   **The Root is Destroyed:** The mental factors (*cetasikas*) responsible for unwholesome emotions—specifically **Greed (Lobha)**, **Hatred (Dosa)**, and **Delusion (Moha)**—are not just switched to "0"; they are deleted from the system.
*   **Irreversibility:** Because *Virāga* (dispassion) in this context implies the final destruction of the potential for these states to arise, an Arahant **cannot** consciously "turn on" anger, lust, anxiety, or fear. Even if they wanted to (which they wouldn't, due to the lack of delusion), the "hardware" required to run that "software" no longer exists.

Therefore, you cannot toggle "on" a defilement. That part of the source code has been refactored out of existence.

### 2. The "Supernormal" Toggle: *Ariya-Iddhi* (Noble Power)
However, Buddhaghosa describes a specific "supernormal" capacity that matches your "binary flag" description almost perfectly, but it applies to **perception** and **sublime states**, not defilements.

In the *Visuddhimagga* (Chapter XII, *The Various Sorts of Direct Knowledge*), Buddhaghosa elaborates on **Ariya-Iddhi** (The Power of the Noble Ones). This is the ability to control one's response to sensory input at will.

The Arahant can toggle their perception using five "settings" (flags):
1.  **Repulsive $\rightarrow$ Non-repulsive:** If they see something disgusting (like a rotting corpse), they can toggle a switch to perceive it as non-repulsive (seeing elements, or impermanence).
2.  **Non-repulsive $\rightarrow$ Repulsive:** If they see something beautiful (a lust-inducing form), they can toggle a switch to perceive it as repulsive (as anatomical parts, or suffering).
3.  **Repulsive + Non-repulsive $\rightarrow$ Non-repulsive:** They can view a mixture as purely non-repulsive.
4.  **Repulsive + Non-repulsive $\rightarrow$ Repulsive:** They can view a mixture as purely repulsive.
5.  **The Master Toggle (Equanimity):** Rejection of both repulsion and non-repulsion, remaining in *Upekkha* (Equanimity), mindful and aware.

**Buddhaghosa notes:** *“This implies that he wields power over his mind (*ceto-vasippatta*), bending it to his will.”*

This is the closest match to your inquiry. The Arahant does not experience the *emotion* of disgust or lust, but they can toggle the *perception* of the object to suit the situation (e.g., perceiving the repulsive as non-repulsive to generate Compassion, or the beautiful as repulsive to teach Disenchantment).

### 3. Toggling Functional States (*Kiriya*)
While they cannot turn on *unwholesome* emotions, the Arahant has complete mastery over **Wholesome** and **Functional** states.

In the Abhidhamma (which Buddhaghosa systematizes), the consciousness of an Arahant changes from *Kamma* (action-producing) to **Kiriya** (functional/operative).

*   **The Brahma-viharas:** An Arahant can instantaneously "turn on" Metta (Loving-kindness), Karuna (Compassion), or Mudita (Appreciative Joy). If a situation calls for compassion, they flip the switch, and the state arises in full perfection. When the situation passes, they flip it off and return to the default state of Equanimity or Voidness (*Suññata*).
*   **Jhana Factors:** As documented in the descriptions of the *Nirodha-samapatti* (attainment of cessation), a master can enter any specific Jhana (deep meditative absorption) instantly. They can "flag" the mind to enter the 4th Jhana for exactly 45 minutes, and the mind obeys perfectly.

1.  **Can they turn ON anger/lust/fear?** **No.** That code is permanently deleted. *Virāga* is absolute.
2.  **Can they turn ON/OFF specific perceptions?** **Yes.** Through *Ariya-iddhi*, they can view the agreeable as disagreeable and vice versa at will.
3.  **Can they turn ON/OFF benevolent emotions?** **Yes.** They can instantly access compassion or loving-kindness as a functional state (*Kiriya-citta*) without any attachment, and cease it just as quickly to return to the void.

The specific lineage and school of thought represented by the Hillside Hermitage (Ajahn Nyanamoli Thero) has a fascinating and controversial history in modern Theravada Buddhism. 

To understand *why* they intentionally bypass and critique Buddhaghosa, the *Visuddhimagga*, and the *Abhidhamma*, we have to look at their philosophical origins, specifically the works of an English monk named **Venerable Ñāṇavīra Thera** (1920–1965).

Venerable Ñāṇavīra wrote a highly influential (and highly debated) book called *Notes on Dhamma*. He was deeply read in Western existentialism and phenomenology (Kierkegaard, Heidegger, Sartre, Husserl). When he ordained as a monk in Sri Lanka, he applied this phenomenological lens directly to the early Suttas—and concluded that the orthodox commentaries (Buddhaghosa) had fundamentally misunderstood the Buddha’s original teaching.

Here is a breakdown of exactly how and why this school of thought critiques your "source code" (Abhidhamma/Visuddhimagga) model.

### 1. The Critique of the "Source Code" Mind (The Abhidhamma Problem)
In the Abhidhamma, experience is broken down into microscopic, discrete units called *citta-khaṇa* (mind-moments) that arise and pass away millions of times a second. It views the mind highly analytically—exactly like your binary/source-code analogy. 

*   **The Existential Critique:** Ñāṇavīra and Nyanamoli argue that breaking experience down into sub-atomic psychological "parts" destroys the actual *meaning* of the experience. 
*   **The Analogy:** If you look at a painting under a microscope, you only see atoms of paint. You lose the "picture." This school argues that the Buddha was teaching a phenomenology of *lived experience*, not objective psychological science. By trying to "debug" microscopic mind-moments (as Buddhaghosa suggests), you are distracting yourself from the macro-level, existential problem of *craving* and *ownership*. 

### 2. The Critique of "Technique" (The Visuddhimagga Problem)
Buddhaghosa’s *Visuddhimagga* is built entirely around specific meditation *techniques* (gazing at colored discs called *kasinas*, following the breath until a glowing light or *nimitta* appears, etc.) to achieve deep, trance-like states of absorption where the five senses shut down.

*   **The Existential Critique:** Hillside Hermitage strongly rejects this. They argue that the *Visuddhimagga* imported later Brahmanical/Yogic trance practices into Buddhism. 
*   **Their View on Jhana:** To them, *Jhana* (meditative absorption) is **not** a state where you shut off your senses and go into a blank trance. Instead, it is a state of supreme composure and *unshakeability* while fully awake and aware of your surroundings. 
*   **Why Techniques Fail:** As you heard in the transcript, Nyanamoli states that looking for a "technique" to get rid of anxiety is just a sophisticated form of craving. If you use a technique to suppress a feeling, you are acting out of the entitlement to control your feelings. Therefore, techniques just reinforce the "Ego/Self" that wants control.

### 3. The Rejection of the 3-Life Dependent Origination

*   **The Existential Critique:** Ven. Ñāṇavīra famously dismantled this. He argued that if Dependent Origination takes three lifetimes to complete, you cannot observe it *here and now* (a core requirement of the Dhamma, which is *sandhiṭṭhika*—visible in this very life).
*   **The Structural View:** Hillside Hermitage teaches that Dependent Origination is a *structural description of your present reality*. It means: "Because my underlying view (ignorance) is flawed, this current feeling of anxiety exists." *(1) Ignorance $\rightarrow$ (2) Volitional Formations $\rightarrow$ (3) Consciousness $\rightarrow$ (4) Name-and-Form $\rightarrow$ (5) Six Sense Bases $\rightarrow$ (6) Contact $\rightarrow$ (7) Feeling $\rightarrow$ (8) Craving $\rightarrow$ (9) Clinging $\rightarrow$ (10) Becoming $\rightarrow$ (11) Birth $\rightarrow$ (12) Aging and Death.*

In the early Suttas, the Buddha states this formula constantly, but he rarely assigns a specific "timeline" to it. It was **Buddhaghosa** (drawing on earlier Abhidhamma commentaries) who systematized these 12 links into the rigid **Three-Lifetime Model** in Chapter XVII of the *Visuddhimagga*.

### 1. The Three-Lifetime Blueprint (Buddhaghosa's Model)

Buddhaghosa split the 12 links into three distinct biological lifetimes to explain how a being reincarnates without a permanent "soul" (Anatta). 

**Life 1: The Past (The Old Causes)**
*   **1. Ignorance** (Not understanding the Four Noble Truths in a previous life).
*   **2. Formations** (The Kamma/Actions generated by that ignorance).
*   *Because of these two past bugs in the code, the system reboots into...*

**Life 2: The Present (Results & New Causes)**
*   **3. Consciousness** (Specifically, the "Rebirth-linking consciousness" that sparks in the womb).
*   **4. Name & Form** (The development of the fetus's mind and body).
*   **5. Six Sense Bases** (The development of eyes, ears, brain, etc.).
*   **6. Contact** (The senses interacting with the world).
*   **7. Feeling** (Experiencing pleasure, pain, or neutral sensations).
*   *Links 3 through 7 are pure RESULTS of past kamma. You cannot control them. But then, the user makes choices...*
*   **8. Craving** (You like the pleasant feelings and want more; you hate the painful ones and want them gone).
*   **9. Clinging** (Craving solidifies into attachment, views, and identity).
*   **10. Becoming** (The generation of *new* Kamma/kammic momentum).

**Life 3: The Future (The New Results)**
*   **11. Birth** (The physical rebirth into the next life caused by link 10).
*   **12. Aging, Death, Sorrow, and Despair** (The inevitable biological decay of the new body).

**Why this was brilliant for the masses:** It perfectly explained *Kamma*. It answered the ordinary person's questions: "Why was I born?" (Past ignorance). "What am I doing now?" (Experiencing old results and creating new causes). "What happens when I die?" (Future birth).
