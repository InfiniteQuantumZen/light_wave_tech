The old lighthouse keeper, Silas, had seen many strange things wash ashore on his remote stretch of coast. Whale bones bleached white as saints, messages in bottles from lands he couldn't pronounce, and once, a single, perfectly preserved child's shoe, which he kept on his mantelpiece and mourned without knowing why.

But nothing prepared him for the Egg.

It had washed up during the fiercest storm of the decade, the waves high enough to kiss the lighthouse windows. Silas found it nestled amongst shattered driftwood and kelp, humming with a faint, resonant frequency that vibrated through the soles of his worn boots.

He tried to move it, but it was impossibly heavy. So, he built a rough shelter of driftwood around it, more to mark its presence than to protect it from the elements. For weeks, it just sat there, pulsing softly. The gulls avoided it. Even the usually relentless crabs gave it a wide berth.

Then, one moonless night, the humming intensified. Silas, woken by the change, hurried out. The Egg was glowing brighter now, the internal light swirling like a tempest in a jar. Fine cracks, like illuminated spiderwebs, began to appear on its surface.

He watched, mesmerized, as the cracks widened. There was no sound of breaking, no shattering. Instead, the shell seemed to *dissolve* along the fracture lines, turning into a fine, glittering dust that was instantly snatched away by the wind.

What emerged was not a creature, not in any way Silas understood.

It was light.
Or rather, a being woven from light.

It unfolded slowly, like a time-lapse flower made of dawn. It had a vaguely humanoid shape, tall and slender, but its limbs were fluid, its edges indistinct, trailing off into shimmering motes. Where a face might have been, there was a brighter concentration of light, a core of pure incandescence that was painful to look at directly, yet drew the eye.

It took a step, and the sand beneath its "foot" did not shift. It seemed to hover an inch above the ground. It raised a luminous arm, and the air around it shimmered with heatless colours Silas had never seen before – hues that vibrated somewhere between violet and pure energy.

Silas, an old man who believed in tides, sensible boots, and the comforting predictability of the lamp’s rotation, felt no fear. Only a profound, bone-deep awe.

The light-being turned its "head" towards him. There were no eyes, but he felt *seen*, assessed not with judgment, but with an ancient, fathomless curiosity.

The lighthouse beam, always strong and steady, now shone with an impossible brilliance, a pure white light shot through with those otherworldly colours. It cut through fog and storm like a divine blade, visible, Silas wagered, from twice the distance it ever had been before.

Silas lived for another twenty years, his mind clear, his body strangely vital. He never spoke of what he saw, knowing no one would believe him. But every night, as he tended the lamp, he felt the presence within it – the quiet, ancient consciousness. And he knew his little stretch of coast was no longer just a warning to ships, but a beacon for something far greater, a silent greeting from a small, blue world to the vast, wondering universe from which such marvels could emerge.
